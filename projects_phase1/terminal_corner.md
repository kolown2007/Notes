### The Trinity as System Architecture

|**Traditional Entity**|**Tech Equivalent**|**Function in the "Terminal Corner"**|
|---|---|---|
|**The Father**|**The Server**|The "Source." It is remote, invisible, and holds all the "Truth" (data). It exists outside your local space but dictates everything within it.|
|**The Son**|**The Client**|The "Manifestation." This is the physical hardware in your corner—the body. It is the interface through which the Server becomes "flesh" or visible to the user.|
|**The Holy Spirit**|**The API**|The "Connector." It is the invisible protocol (the signal/code) that allows the Server and Client to speak. It is the "breath" or the "flow" of data that makes the system alive.|

1. Shinto and Techno-Animism



This is the core of the **Terminal Corner** critique: the shift from **Communal Faith** to **Individualized Labor**.

In the traditional Filipino home, the altar was a collective anchor for the family’s spiritual safety. Now, the WFH "Terminal" is a solitary portal. You sit in your corner, and through the mediation of an algorithm, your physical presence in a local room is converted into value for a global market.

----
The **Terminal Corner** serves as a contemporary reconstruction of the traditional Filipino house altar, reimagined as the primary interface for the "Work-From-Home" era. In the Philippine domestic landscape, where the sacred is historically elevated above the communal floor, this installation documents the spatial and psychological shift from ancestral devotion to algorithmic labor.
